My research lies at the intersection of business, technology, causal inference, and analytical modeling. I adopt a quantitative approach, combining empirical analysis with theoretical and analytical modeling to study complex economic and technological phenomena.

A central focus of my work is the application of causal machine learning methods in marketing. In this context, I investigate how different marketing interventions affect user behavior, aiming to uncover causal relationships and improve decision-making.

In addition, I study how emerging technologies disrupt established markets through analytical modeling. For example, I analyze how blockchain technology enables enforceable resale fees and how such mechanisms reshape market dynamics.

I am also involved in the Volkswagen Foundation–funded project *“From Machine Learning to Machine Teaching.”* Within this project, I apply machine learning and statistical methods in medical contexts, including research on post-COVID conditions and community-acquired pneumonia.
